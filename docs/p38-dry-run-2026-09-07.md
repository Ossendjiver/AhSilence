## What worked

The microphone-only detector did find repeatable P38-relevant narrowband structure. Both cold and warm adaptive dry runs completed, with 5,114 full-band discovery scans. The cold run admitted 13 lanes and reached its first modelled `RUNNING` state after 162.5 seconds. The dry runner reported a best modelled reduction of 23.57 dB and a mean modelled reduction of 9.24 dB while a lane was actually running.

Those reduction figures are controller-model results only. The dry runner used a synthetic unity-gain, 50 ms secondary path; they are not predictions of acoustic cancellation in the vehicle. Recorded spectral levels are dBFS, not calibrated cabin SPL.

## Failure modes exposed

The principal problem was not detection but lane ownership and calibration continuity.

- `RUNNING` coverage was only 0.0144% of controller time.
- There were 16,412 baseline-refresh updates attributed to target movement during calibration.
- 25,512 of 25,559 controller ticks (99.816%) contained two or more lanes within 1 Hz of each other.
- 45,390 lane-update rows were at the 8 Hz lower bound.
- Replaying learned recipes did not improve the dry run: reported recalibrations increased from 22 in the cold pass to 33 in the warm pass.

The dry-run package therefore concluded that independently admitted lanes were converging onto the same physical spectral line and that small tracker movements repeatedly invalidated in-flight baseline/path measurements before controllers could sustain `RUNNING`.

## v0.5.8 response

The first code response deliberately keeps the existing output ceilings and verification safeguards. It does not loosen acceptance thresholds merely to increase dry-run `RUNNING` time.

1. **Calibration retune hysteresis** — small frequency changes no longer reset an in-flight baseline/path probe. A materially larger move still mutes and refreshes the baseline.
2. **Controller-centred exact analysis** — while hysteresis holds a probe frequency, the exact residual measurement follows the controller-owned frequency rather than the raw tracker estimate.
3. **Bounded discovered-lane tracking** — microphone-discovered lanes are constrained to a neighbourhood around the frequency at which the physical line was admitted.
4. **Collision coalescing** — if two independently admitted fallback lanes later converge within the collision radius, the older lane retains ownership and the duplicate is stopped/removed.
5. **Broadband exclusion alignment** — the vehicle bank now exposes controller-owned lane frequencies so the speculative broadband path excludes the tone actually being controlled.
6. **Regression tests** — tests cover bounded tracking and calibration retune hysteresis.

## Follow-up validation

The next useful comparison is the same full-WAV dry-run procedure against `0.5.8-rebuild`, focusing on:

- baseline-refresh count versus 16,412;
- collision-tick percentage versus 99.816%;
- `RUNNING` coverage versus 0.0144%;
- cold versus warm recalibration count;
- whether any discovered lanes remain pinned at 8 Hz without an admitted physical line near that boundary;
- lane identity through sustained frequency drift.

Only after those controller metrics improve should learned-recipe keying be expanded. The dry-run report specifically recommends tying recipes to a physical source plus speed/RPM bin rather than to a drifting frequency alone. The report also recommends making lanes below a verified speaker-path capability monitor-only; that remains a separate route/secondary-path validation change rather than being inferred from this dry run.
